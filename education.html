University of Rhode Island

BA in History; BA in Art History (German; Gender Studies Minors)

When I began at URI as an in-state student, I was slightly apprehensive about college and almost totally unsure what to pursue there. My majors as declared on my application were Studio Art and History. Gradually, Studio Art shifted to Art History, and I added a minor in German as well as Gender and Women's Studies. I also began with the impression that I would become a professional artist, as I was a child prodigy in drawing. However, my interests quickly shifted almost entirely to history and art history, and as a result my entire self-perception of my skills, abilities, and potential radically shifted. I enrolled in an upper-level history class as a freshman with special permission, participated in a research fellowship in my second year, then a graduate level course in my third-year, and finally a graduate level research internship. I was fortunate to receive a scholarship and study abroad in Austria, although I realized there that German history was not my primary interest. However, the hard work of language-learning proved to be a valuable supplement to studying the history of psychiatry, especially as someone interested in psychoanalysis. I graduated having received an award in German Studies, an award from the History Department, and the annual Undergraduate Humanities Excellence Award awarded by the university's Center for the Humanities.

University of Massachusetts Amherst

MA in History; Graduate Certificate in Public History

I launched directly into my Master's program at UMass Amherst directly after graduating from URI. I was guaranteed funding for all two years on the condition that I serve as a teaching assistant. This was quite frightening, as I had never taught in my life, let alone presented something in front of a group of 60 or more people. After a tumultuous first semester teaching Colonial Latin America, I finally landed on my feet that spring in the class History of Baseball. Although I did not know much of anything about baseball going in, its modern U.S.-focus made me more comfortable and confident, and I drastically improved my competency with students. The coursework also proved to be a big change, but I've found that I love diving into deep research, doing academic readings, and improving my writing. I received a department award for my historiography paper on the history of psychiatry, and that summer I worked on reading and annotating more prominent books in the field. In addition, I was awarded a fellowship to spend the summer at the New Bedford Whaling Museum working with their collections and curatorial teams to catalogue photographs and contribute research to an upcoming permanent exhibit. My last year is dedicated to my thesis on the history of psychiatric twin studies and looking for post-graduate opportunities in museums, libraries, archives, historical societies, and nonprofits.
